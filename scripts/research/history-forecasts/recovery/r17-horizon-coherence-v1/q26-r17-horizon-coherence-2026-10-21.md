## Mathematical guarantee and its boundary

For one origin/barrier let C={q in R^5:q1<=...<=q5}, and q be the Euclidean projection of p onto C. PAV averages violating adjacent blocks. Inputs in [0,1] therefore stay in [0,1]. It uses predictions only, never outcomes. If nested first-hit labels y belong to C, projection optimality gives <p-q,y-q><=0 and hence

    sum_H (q_H-y_H)^2 <= sum_H (p_H-y_H)^2 - sum_H (p_H-q_H)^2.

This holds separately for every fully labeled origin/barrier and for any fixed collection of those complete vectors, including pooled/year/asset subsets. It does not require exchangeability, independence, calibration or a statistical test. It is a property of the specified target/loss coordinates, not independent evidence of forecast skill.

Individual-H Brier can worsen. Exact two-coordinate example: p=(.9,.1), y=(1,1), q=(.5,.5). The short-H loss rises .01 to .25, while total loss falls .82 to .50. Embed it among five coordinates to obtain the same counterexample. If y is not nested, the guarantee fails: p=y=(1,0) has zero loss before projection and .5 total loss afterward. If one label is missing and only its counterpart is scored, even true nested latent labels do not ensure the observed subset loss improves. No guarantee is claimed for an H-dependent weighted mix of view/cohort memberships.

Severity order is preserved if precisely the same five-H projection and equal weights are used for every barrier at an origin. Isotonic regression is coordinatewise order preserving: q_i=max_{a<=i} min_{b>=i} average(p_a,...,p_b). If p at a deeper barrier is <=p at a shallower barrier for every H, every interval average and thus every min/max is ordered. Therefore applying this map to each barrier preserves severity monotonicity. Different missing-H subsets, weights or projections across barriers would break this argument and are prohibited. This supplies a monotone grid of first-hit marginals; it does not validate probabilities, continuous-time path behavior or actual liquidation/oracle mechanics.
