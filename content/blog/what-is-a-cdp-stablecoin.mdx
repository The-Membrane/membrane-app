You own crypto. You need dollars — for an expense, an opportunity, or just to
stop being 100% exposed for a while. The obvious move is to sell. A CDP
stablecoin exists so that selling is not the only move.

## What is a collateralized debt position?

A **collateralized debt position** (CDP) is a vault inside a smart contract.
You deposit an asset the protocol accepts as collateral, and the protocol
lets you mint — literally create — a dollar-pegged token against it. On
Membrane that token is **CDT**. The vault records your collateral and your
debt, and the position stays open for as long as you want: there is no fixed
term and no repayment schedule. Repay the CDT, and the tokens are burned and
your collateral unlocks.

That is the whole primitive. Everything else — interest rates, liquidation
rules, oracles — is machinery to keep the borrowed token worth a dollar
while people do this at scale.

## Why borrow against crypto instead of selling it?

Two reasons, one financial and one practical.

The financial reason: selling ends your exposure. If you sell to raise cash
and the asset then doubles, the cash cost you the upside. Borrowing keeps
the position: your collateral stays yours, still in the vault, still
exposed, while you spend the borrowed dollars.

The practical reason: in many jurisdictions, selling is a taxable disposal
and taking a loan is not. That difference is jurisdiction-dependent, it has
edge cases (a liquidation may have its own tax treatment), and nothing here
is tax advice — but it is a real part of why this mechanism exists.

Borrowing is not free of downside, and it is worth saying plainly before
any more benefits: you are leveraged, and if your collateral falls far
enough, the protocol will sell part of it whether you like it or not. The
rest of this post is mostly about exactly when and how that happens.

## How does the stablecoin stay backed?

Every CDT in circulation was minted against collateral worth more than the
debt — the protocol requires **overcollateralization** at all times. You
cannot mint $100 of CDT against $100 of collateral; you mint it against
meaningfully more, and the protocol enforces a maximum ratio between your
debt and your collateral value.

When a position's collateral falls too close to its debt, the protocol
liquidates: it repays debt using the position's collateral before the debt
can exceed what backs it. Backing, in other words, is not a promise — it is
a rule with an enforcement mechanism attached.

At launch, Membrane's onboarded collateral is a basket of yield-bearing
dollar assets (sUSDS, syrupUSDC, scrvUSD), with a broader basket of majors
planned after deploy.

## What do LTV, the borrow cap, and the liquidation line mean?

**LTV** — loan-to-value — is your debt divided by your collateral's current
value. Deposit $1,000 and borrow 500 CDT, and your LTV is 50%. It moves
whenever your collateral price moves.

Every market has two lines above you:

- **The liquidation line (M)** — the maximum LTV a position may reach. Cross it and the position is in breach.
- **The borrow cap (B)** — the maximum LTV at which you can *open* new debt. On Membrane this is a fixed relationship: `B = M − 3%`. The 3% gap is a protocol constant, not a per-market knob.

The gap means you cannot borrow yourself directly onto the liquidation
line. A position opened at the cap starts with a 3% cushion by
construction. As a worked example with our real launch parameters: the
sUSDS market's liquidation line is 88%, so its borrow cap is 85%.

## What happens if I cross the liquidation line?

This is where Membrane differs most from the venues you may know. Crossing
the line does not liquidate you. It starts a timer.

- **Cross the line** → an **8-hour window** opens. During it you can save the position three ways: deposit more collateral, repay some debt, or simply have the price recover. Any of the three, at any point inside the window.
- **Fall 4% past the line** (LTV ≥ M × 1.04) → the window is skipped and liquidation is immediate. The window is protection against ordinary volatility, not against a collapse.
- **Window expires unsaved** → liquidation runs, and it is **partial**: it repays your debt down to the borrow cap, not to zero. You lose the collateral needed to cover that repayment plus a liquidation fee; the rest of the position survives, back at a safe LTV.

Liquidation is still the thing to avoid — it sells your collateral at a bad
moment and charges a fee for the privilege. But the failure mode is "lose a
slice, keep the position," not "wake up to nothing."

## What does borrowing cost?

Interest on the debt. Membrane's borrow rate launched at a **3% base rate**
and is adaptive — it moves with market conditions, so the number to trust
is the live one on the [borrow page](/ethereum/mint), not a number in a
blog post. There is no fixed term and no early-repayment penalty; interest
accrues while the debt is open.

## What can go wrong?

Ranked by likelihood, not by comfort:

1. **Price risk.** Your collateral falls, your LTV crosses the line, and you are into the 8-hour window — or past the 4% threshold and liquidated immediately. This is the ordinary way CDP borrowers lose money, and no parameter removes it.
2. **Oracle risk.** The protocol acts on prices from oracles — Membrane's primary source is Uniswap V3 TWAPs, with Chainlink feeds as a secondary cross-check. A wrong price means wrong liquidation decisions. Multiple sources reduce this; nothing eliminates it.
3. **Collateral risk.** The launch collateral is yield-bearing dollar tokens, which carry the risk of their own underlying protocols. A problem in the venue behind your collateral is a problem for your position, independent of anything Membrane does.
4. **Smart contract risk.** The vault, the liquidation engine, and the token are code. Code can have bugs. This is true of every DeFi protocol and does not get a pass here.

## Provenance

Every parameter above traces to the Membrane contracts (read 2026-08-24):
the `B = M − 3%` gap (`Constants.sol`, `BORROW_LTV_GAP = 3e16`), the 8-hour
window (`LiquidationEngine.sol`, `28800` seconds), the 4% immediate
threshold and the sUSDS 88% line (deploy configuration), partial
liquidation to the borrow cap (`LiquidationEngine.sol`, restore-to-B), the
3% launch base rate (deploy configuration), and the oracle hierarchy
(`UniswapV3Adapter.sol`, `ChainlinkAdapter.sol`). Parameters are
governance-adjustable; the app always shows the live values.
