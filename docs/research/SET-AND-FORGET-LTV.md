# Set-and-forget LTV: which start LTV survives being left alone

Owner ask (2026-10-04): "what LTV per asset will be profitable to set and forget with our recall mech". **This replay answers only the survival half: the start LTV at which collateral is never sold. It does not answer "profitable"**, see [Not answered: profitable](#not-answered-profitable). Generated by `scripts/position-sim/set-and-forget-sim.ts`: first run 2026-10-06, venue analogs re-measured after a data review and the exit-capacity model changed to cash vs book on 2026-10-07 ([Method](#method)). Every number below is in `public/data/price-history/set-and-forget-ltv.json`, including which levels have a book larger than the venue (`mechanics.exitCapacity.analogs[].bookExceedsVenue`, with the same window's floor `fWindow`); the same rows are generated into `lib/position-sim/exitCapacityAnalogs.ts`. **Historical replay, not a forecast and not a probability.**

**Exit capacity: cash vs book is the headline, pro-rata is the floor (owner ruling 2026-10-07).** A carry position's recall is paid from the venue's idle cash. The headline asks, for each real stress event at a real venue (2023-10 → 2026-10): did the idle cash that venue had over the first 8 hours cover what **Membrane's whole book at that venue** would recall? The multiple is m = min(1, idle cash ÷ book), measured at a **$10M, $50M and $250M book**; every claim below names its book. It rests on four assumptions:

1. **The whole book recalls at once**, at the onset. Conservative: in practice only positions that cross their line recall, and only down to their borrow LTV.
2. **The observed cash is first come within the hour, and already net of everyone else who withdrew in that hour.** It is what was left after them. This does not hold for the Steakhouse vault: its cash is the vault's pro-rata share of each Morpho market's idle cash, not cash it can claim first, so its levels are conservative on (2).
3. **Membrane's recall does not itself start a run** on the venue.
4. **The engine's stock never refills across the horizon.** A venue that recovers after the window is not credited.

The **floor** is the earlier pro-rata model: every depositor races for the exit at the same moment and a recall gets its pro-rata share, cash ÷ everything deposited. On the same event it never pays more than cash vs book for any book the venue could hold (a book no larger than the venue's supply). For a book larger than the venue's whole supply in that window, the observed history is not an analog: if Membrane's deposit added to the supply, the venue would have been a different venue. Those levels are marked ([Floor](#exit-capacity-measured-venue-analogs)). It is not what depositors could actually withdraw in those events.

- **Every recall draws on a stock that never refills across the horizon** (assumption 4). A second line crossing in the same horizon finds only what the first left.

## Exit capacity: measured venue analogs

Owner instruction (2026-10-06): "the venue-capacity assumptions should directly analogize existing protocols (assuming typical Aave capacity during stress over the last 3 years)"; owner ruling (2026-10-07): the headline is cash vs book, pro-rata is the floor. The hand-picked ×0.5 "stressed" and ×0.1 "kelp-lock" levels are gone, and so are the remaining hand-picked multipliers: the Risk Frontier grid's capacity rows are now cuts measured from the default level (Aave USDC typical at a $50M book: its bad event, ×0.9032, and its everyone-exits floor, ×0.1119), and its tree's capacity branch runs the everyone-exits floor of the venue and level the position chose (×0.1119 at the default). Method: `lib/position-sim/venueStressAnalogs.ts` (`cashVsBookAnalogs`, `venueStressAnalogs`; reading rules shared with the builder in `venueStressRules.ts`). Engine presets: `lib/position-sim/exitCapacityAnalogs.ts`. Readings: `public/data/venue-stress/` (archive reads of each venue's cash and supply, every hour). Every level below is also in the JSON (`mechanics.exitCapacity.analogs`).

- **The readings.** Cash: Aave/Spark `underlying.balanceOf(aToken)`; for the Steakhouse MetaMorpho vault, its pro-rata share of each market's idle cash. Supply: `aToken.totalSupply()` / `totalAssets()`. A paused reserve counts as no cash; a frozen one still allows withdrawals. Stablecoins are held at $1; WETH cash is priced at the ETH hourly close.
- **Per event.** The idle cash is the lowest over the first 8 h after the onset (the delay window), with an hourly keeper retry: a one-reading dip the next hourly reading clears does not set the minimum. Windows whose onsets chain within 72 h form one event, counted once at its worst trigger.
- **Cash vs book (headline).** m = min(1, idle cash ÷ book). Per venue and per book, events are ranked on m (every m ≤ 1% ties as locked and ranks by lock length), and **typical** = the median event, **bad** = the 10th percentile (nearest rank), **worst** = the worst observed. Each level is one real event. A larger book never gets a higher m (tested on the data and on a synthetic sweep).
- **Floor (pro-rata).** f = cash ÷ supply, ranked the same way: the share each depositor would get if all raced at once. On the same event f ≤ m for any book no larger than the venue's supply (cash ÷ B ≥ cash ÷ supply).
- **Book exceeds the venue.** Where m < f on the same event, the book is larger than the venue's whole supply in that window. For a book that large the observed history is not an analog: if Membrane's deposit added to the supply, the venue would have been a different venue. That is 9 of the 90 cash-vs-book levels, all at a $250M book: Steakhouse USDC typical / bad / worst, Spark USDC typical / bad / worst, Spark USDS bad / worst and Aave USDe worst. They stay in the picker, marked "book exceeds the venue" (`bookExceedsVenue` in the JSON's `mechanics.exitCapacity.analogs`, next to the same window's floor `fWindow`; the same rows in `exitCapacityAnalogs.ts`), and they are the only levels that pay less than the floor on the same event. Aave USDC, the venue the sim uses, has none. Comparing a venue's levels across the two models can compare different events: at Spark DAI's worst level both models are locked (≤ 1%) and differ only in the lock and inside the 1% band.
- **Onto the engine.** exit capacity = deployed × m (or f). For a **locked** level (≤ 1%) the venue also returns nothing for the measured lock, counted from the first breach: the ≤ 1% stretch the 8 h window runs into (cash under 1% of the book, or f under 1%), measured to its end, so starting it at the breach is early by at most 8 h. Recovery after a lock is not credited.
- **Bounds kept.** ×0 "frozen" (a paused reserve, or a recall that loses the race) and ×1 "optimistic", an **upper bound, never a default** (everything comes back in every scenario). A measured cash-vs-book level can itself be ×1, where the idle cash covered the whole book (at Aave USDC: the $10M book typical and bad, the $50M book typical); its label then says so.

## Method

- **Start hours:** every hour whose whole window is in the data (stride 1h, no skipping). ETH and BTC: 2020-01-01 → 2026-10-06; ETH 58,562 / 57,122 / 50,522 and BTC 58,563 / 57,123 / 50,523 starts for 30 / 90 / 365d. wstETH starts 2021-02-20; weETH starts 2023-11-10.
- **Path:** each hour is two 30-minute steps: its oracle low, then its close, relative to the entry close. Lows are rounds the oracle printed (a keeper could act on them). Highs are left out (they could clear a timer and flatter the window). The price history carries the Chainlink phase-handover round (`lib/position-sim/oracleRounds.ts`).
- **Engine:** every "sold?" is one stressGrid `runStress` replay. Rules: delayed class; recall before sale; 3pp borrow gap; $2,000 `liqDebtMinimum`; the owner's intended rules (recall asks loan − B × collateral; no sub-minimum dust); $100k collateral. Carry deploys the whole debt at a named `EXIT_CAPACITY_PRESETS` level: a measured cash-vs-book or floor level, or a bound. Levered long has no recall.
- **Solve:** per start hour, frontier.ts `bisectEdge` solves the start LTV to 0.02pp. The search runs between line × the window's lowest ratio (below it nothing crosses the line) and the cap. Starts are visited in order of that bound, and the walk stops once no unvisited start can enter the lowest 5%, so the tail is exact. "≤ f" = the ⌊f·n⌋-th smallest edge, rounded down to 0.1pp.
- **Cash-vs-book run (2026-10-07):** 33 rows newly solved: on ETH in both classes Aave USDC $50M bad, $250M typical and bad, and $10M and $50M worst; on ETH delayed Steakhouse $50M typical. That is 1,820,088 engine runs and 22.1 CPU-minutes (the rows' `seconds`, one process at a time). The A1 claim measured 40 rows (four new venue stocks × five sizes × two horizons): 440,740 runs, 12.9 CPU-minutes. A locked level (≤ 1%) starts its bisection from the levered-long bracket, which the engine verifies before using it.
- **Rows carried over:** the other 303 of the 336 rows are seeded by venue stock from the published 2026-10-07 pro-rata run (`seededFrom`): a case whose stock (multiple and lock) matches a published row gets that row, under its own id (`seededAs` when the source case had another id: the floor levels were published as `carry:aave-usdc-typical` / `-bad` / `-worst`, and every ×1 cash-vs-book level is the published `carry:optimistic` row). Levered long is seeded by id. In the A1 claim, the floor and levered-long rows are copied the same way.
- **Copy claims:** a claim quoted at one LTV is measured directly, not read off the ℓ\* table. That is one `runStress` per start hour at that LTV (`tallyAtLtv`, driver `--claims`), at several position sizes, with no bisection and so no monotonicity assumed. These runs also record how often recall fired and how much of the deployed debt it unwound (`claims` in the JSON).
- **Checks:**
  - Across every cell, the worst windows were probed at each whole % between the bound and the solved edge: no sale in 45,895 probes, and no bound violation.
  - The analog rows, both models, are recomputed from the venue history and must match `exitCapacityAnalogs.ts` exactly (`tests/unit/venueStressAnalogs.test.ts`), which also checks that a larger book never gets a higher m.
  - Unit tests in `tests/unit/setAndForget.test.ts`, including a brute-force engine scan over a locked measured level.

## Assumptions

- **Lines:**
  - ETH 80% is master's `WETH_MAX_LTV` (DeployFullSystem.s.sol:212, "placeholder; re-derive per risk model"). 90% is its delayed-class listing cap, not the line.
  - BTC 75% (WBTC entry), wstETH 78% and weETH 75% are **modelled** `MEMBRANE_ASSET_LTV` entries, not protocol state.
  - All four list in the delayed class; no-delay is for stables.
  - Levered-long edges scale with the line (ℓ*/line does not depend on the line, tested): at a 90% ETH line, multiply by 1.125.
- **LSTs:** ETH/USD × exchange rate (`stEthPerToken`, `getRate`). No market depeg.
- **Exit capacity (headline): cash vs book**, m = min(1, the venue's lowest idle cash over the first 8 h of one real stress event ÷ Membrane's whole book at the venue), at a stated book. Assumptions (1)–(4) at the top: the whole book recalls at once; the observed cash is first come within the hour and net of other withdrawers (not for the Steakhouse vault, whose cash is its pro-rata share of each market's idle cash: conservative there); the recall starts no run; the stock never refills across the horizon.
- **Exit capacity (floor): pro-rata**, every depositor exits at once and a recall gets cash ÷ supply of its deposit.
- **Locks:** a locked level (≤ 1%) also gives no recall for its measured lock, from the first breach. Recovery after a lock is not credited.
- **Bounds:** ×1 is an upper bound only. ×0 is the theoretical lower bound (a paused reserve, or a recall that loses the race).

## Caveats

- **Past windows ≠ future ones.** Starts overlap: the 365-day tail rests on ≈6 years and one bear market (Nov-2021 → Jun-2022).
- **The analogs describe 2023-10 → 2026-10 only.** The price windows reach back to 2020, but the venue history does not. Aave USDC's events start 2024-01. The 2020–2022 crashes are run at capacity measured in later, different stress.
  - **Thin samples.** Spark USDC, USDS and USDT and Aave USDe have 9–14 events each, so their "bad" level is rank 1–2.
  - **A level is one event,** not a distribution. Aave USDT's floor "bad" event (2026-10-06) runs past the data's end.
- **Cash vs book rests on its four assumptions.** The book is a hypothetical: Membrane is not on mainnet, so no book exists at any venue. (1) is conservative. (2) and (3) are not: if Membrane's own recall were the withdrawal that drained the venue, or set off others, the cash it found would be smaller than the cash observed, and the floor is the answer to that case. Where the idle cash covered all or most of the book (at Aave USDC: the $10M and $50M books in the typical and bad events, the $250M book in the typical event) the result is "cap", so the claim moves with the book: name it.
- **The floor is a modelling choice too.** Depositors did not all exit at once in these events; the cash stayed on the venue for whoever came first. The figures are each window's lowest on the keeper-retry basis (each hour counts as the better of that hour and the next), so with an hourly retry at least that much was available: at least $231.1M in the floor's typical Aave USDC event (ETH −13.3% 2026-06-05), at least $802.0M in the $50M book's typical event (ETH −29.3% 2025-02-02), and as little as $7.1k at Kelp. Raw single-hour readings dip lower (e.g. $215.3M at the 2026-06-05 onset hour).
- **Neither model counts** suppliers re-entering, rate-driven repayment inside the window, or the recall's own size moving the market.
- **Not modelled:**
  - keeper/protocol fees, gas, slippage, MEV;
  - interest accrual (pushes LTV up over long holds);
  - venue yield;
  - the stale-timer amnesty;
  - the venue stock refilling across the horizon.
- **Horizon end:** a timer armed near the end resolves on the held last price and counts (BTC 365d, weETH 90d: sold 7.5h after the horizon). That is conservative by at most the band.
- **Never sold ≠ untouched:** in carry, recall drew down the deployed capital on every line crossing ("cap" rows above; at HF 2 recall fired in 8.53% / 33.98% of start hours).
- **Debt floor (size):** at $100k of collateral the $2,000 `liqDebtMinimum` never decides whether a sale happens. Near the floor it does:
  - **The mechanism.** A recall that would leave less than $2,000 of debt asks for the whole loan. The venue returns m of it, and the rest is closed by selling collateral at the first line crossing, with no window (sale reason `floor`).
  - **Measured at HF 2** (cash vs book at Aave USDC). At ×0.9032 (the $50M book bad) and ×0.8125 (the $250M book typical), $3,999.60 of debt was floor-sold in 8.53% (90d) / 33.98% (365d) of start hours, more than a levered long (7.35% / 32.62%); at ×0.1806 (the $250M book bad) it was not, and at ×1 (the $10M book typical and bad, the $50M book typical) the venue returned the whole loan and nothing was sold ([Copy claims](#copy-claims-measured-per-position-size)).
  - **Monotonicity.** Small positions also break the bisection's monotonicity. **The ℓ\* tables hold for large positions only.**

## Not answered: profitable

The owner asked which LTV is **profitable** to set and forget. Every number in this doc answers only "was collateral sold". No income or cost enters it, so none of it shows a position made money. Missing:

- **Income:** venue yield on the deployed debt, less Membrane's curator-set share of it. Not modelled.
- **The fee on a recall cure:** on master, a recall-only cure is still billed the ordinary liquidation fee on the restore target (`LiquidationEngine.sol` STEP 2.5, ~1623–1658), and it is paid from collateral. The engine omits it (`stressGrid.ts` header, OMITTED). So a carry position that is "never sold" still loses some collateral each time recall fires.
- **Unwound carry:** for ETH carry at HF 2 with $40,000 of debt at Aave USDC, recall fired in 8.53% of start hours over 90 days and 33.98% over a year. It drew a median 17.2% / 30.0% of the deployed debt under cash vs book at the $10M and $50M books (typical and bad) and the $250M book typical; 17.2% / 18.1% at the $250M book bad (×0.1806, capped by the venue's 18.1%); and the floor's share on the floor (11.19% typical, 3.91% bad). Capital that is drawn stops earning.
- **Interest** on a levered long (see Caveats). Deployed carry debt is charged through the venue, not as interest.

The table answers "survives", not "profits". A profitability model needs the measured inputs (how often recall fires and how much it unwinds); they are in `claims` in the JSON.

## What this means for copy
